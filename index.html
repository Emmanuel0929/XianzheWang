About Me

Welcome! My name is Xianzhe Wang (English: Emanuel; Chinese: 王宪喆; nickname: 小戏). I am a Ph.D. student at the University of California, Davis, working at the intersection of operations research, transportation systems, and artificial intelligence.

My research focuses on how to turn data and predictions into reliable and executable decisions. I am particularly interested in integrating machine learning with mathematical optimization, where system structure informs learning, data refine our understanding of the system, and optimization translates information into action. My current interests include decision-aware learning and estimation, stochastic and sequential optimization, decomposition methods, and learning-augmented optimization, with applications in transportation, logistics, and supply chain systems. I value research that is not only mathematically interesting, but also computationally efficient and implementable in practice.

Beyond operations research, I have a long-standing interest in artificial intelligence, particularly large language models, AI agents, and emerging AI technologies. I previously worked as an editor at Xiaoyao Technology, a leading AI technology media outlet in China, where I wrote and edited nearly 100 original articles on AI research, emerging technologies, paper reviews, and industry developments. My work has reached more than 200,000 cumulative readers, with selected articles shared and republished by university research groups. I have also contributed technical and industry analyses for organizations including Baidu, Alibaba, OPPO, and Zhiyuan.

More broadly, I hope to bridge optimization, machine learning, and real-world decision-making, developing methods that retain strong mathematical foundations while remaining practical, scalable, and reliable in real-world systems.

Publications & Working Papers

Decision-Aware Origin-Destination Demand Estimation with Structurally Constrained Data

- Xianzhe Wang, Yueyue Fan*, Johannes O. Royset

- Under review at Transportation Science

- This paper studies an origin-destination (OD) demand estimation problem that explicitly considers the impact of estimation errors on downstream decision quality. Specifically, a decision-aware estimation framework is developed to characterize an admissible demand region using structurally constrained traffic data and identify OD estimates with low downstream decision regret. A geometric representation of the feasible demand region and a convex regret surrogate are further introduced to make the resulting optimization problem computationally tractable.

Solving Non-stationary Non-Markovian Queues: A Transfer Learning Neural Network Approach

- Spyros Garyfallos, Yunan Liu*, Xianzhe Wang, Pere Barlet-Ros, Alberto Cabellos-Aparicio

- Under review at INFORMS Journal on Computing

- This paper studies performance analysis and capacity planning for nonstationary non-Markovian queueing systems with time-varying arrivals, staffing, and general service and abandonment distributions. Specifically, a neural network framework, QUANT, is developed to learn the complex spatio-temporal behavior of queueing systems and efficiently adapt to new environments through transfer learning. The learned model enables fast and accurate performance prediction and can be directly integrated with optimization models for staffing and capacity planning.

Dynamic repositioning in bike-sharing systems with uncertain demand: An improved rolling horizon framework

- Xiang Li, Xianzhe Wang, Ziyan Feng*

- Omega-International Journal Of Management Science

- This paper studies a dynamic bike repositioning problem under scenario-based demand to achieve a balance in inventory levels for electronic fences. Specifically, a multi-period two-stage stochastic model is proposed to make the trade-off between operational costs and service quality. The model incorporates a vehicle no-return (VNR) strategy, which allows vehicles to remain at the last station at the end of each period, awaiting the next repositioning task

- Made oral presentation in Fifth Annual Conference of the Intelligent Decision and Game Branch of CSOOPEM.

A parallel genetic algorithm with variable neighborhood search for the vehicle routing problem in forest fire-fighting

- Xiang Li, Nan Chen, Hongguang Ma*, Fapeng Nie, Xianzhe Wang

- IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems

- This paper investigates the route optimization for fire-fighting vehicles and formulates a mixed integer linear programming model to minimize the total losses of overall fire spots. A parallel genetic algorithm with variable neighborhood search (PGA-VNS) is developed, which includes a parallelized evolution process of multiple sub-populations for ensuring the fast convergency to a high-quality solution, and a multi-operator variable searching process to promote the convergency to a nearly optimal solution.
